Hi,

We're sending a customer follow-up email series on the dealership's behalf, from
Northcountryrecalls@bobjohnsonauto.com. For that mail to authenticate properly rather
than be treated as spoofed, our sending platform needs to be authorised to sign for
the domain.

That's four DNS records. Nothing else is needed from you.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT ASK FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Worth saying up front, because it's a smaller request than it sounds:

  - No access to any mailbox. Nobody gains the ability to read, send from,
    or sign in to a Microsoft 365 account.
  - No app registration, no OAuth consent, no Microsoft admin approval.
  - No change to how your existing mail flows. Inbound and outbound mail
    through Microsoft 365 is untouched.

These records only tell receiving mail servers that one specific platform is
permitted to sign mail for the domain.


PLEASE DO NOT CHANGE YOUR EXISTING SPF RECORD
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Your root domain almost certainly has an SPF record today, something like:

  v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all

Please leave it exactly as it is.

A domain may only have ONE SPF record. If a second is added to the root, SPF
fails for the whole domain and your normal business email starts going to spam.

None of the records below touch it. The SPF entries here go on a "send"
subdomain that doesn't exist yet, which is specifically why the request is
built this way.


THE FOUR RECORDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The "Name" values are the short form most DNS panels expect. If your panel
wants a fully qualified name instead, use the FQDN line. Please don't enter
both, or you'll end up with bobjohnsonauto.com.bobjohnsonauto.com.
