# Claims audit

Every offer fact carried in the creative, checked line by line against the
executed customer contract: form **`AAS VSC 1 11-2022`**, (c) 2022 Ascent
Administration Services, LLC, supplied as
`source-documents/Elevate-Platinum-VSC-Contract.pdf`.

The Vista build found that the design handoff had the administrator's city wrong
and named no obligor at all. The same class of error was waiting here. This is
what the contract actually supports.

## Naming

"Elevate" is the Ascent product brand that DriveOne Direct private labels.
Confirmed by Ryan, 27 Aug 2026. The word does not appear anywhere in the
sixteen pages of the contract, which names its tiers **Silver, Gold and
Platinum**. So "Elevate Platinum" is a program name and is never a contract
citation. Do not print it as though it were the contract's own name.

## Corrected

### "Any ASE-certified shop nationwide"

**Removed.** The string "ASE" does not appear anywhere in the contract. Ryan
confirmed the change on 27 Aug 2026.

The contract's actual term is better than the claim it replaced, and carries a
condition the claim omitted:

> Your Vehicle must be at a Repair Facility, **of Your choosing**, within the
> United States ... Your Repair Facility must call Us at 866-660-7003 to open a
> claim **BEFORE any repairs have begun**. (VII)

with Repair Facility defined as "a licensed Repair Facility (licensed as a
retail merchant to perform mechanical repairs) authorized by the
Administrator/Obligor."

Two things were wrong with the old claim. It narrowed a broad right ("any
licensed facility you choose") into a narrower sounding certification the
contract never mentions, and it dropped prior authorization, which is the single
condition most likely to produce a denied claim and an angry customer. The
creative now says "any licensed repair facility in the U.S." with the
authorization requirement next to it.

### Repair cost figures

The alternator range holds at **$806 to $1,087**. The transmission range has
drifted: RepairPal now shows labor $1,112 to $1,632 and parts around $5,053,
which is **$6,165 to $6,685**, against the $6,003 to $6,525 previously carried.
Verified 27 Aug 2026. The creative uses the current range and dates it on the
frame, per the standing rule that repair figures must be current and dated.

### Cancellation window

The base contract gives **thirty** days for a full refund, not sixty:

> If You cancel Your Service Contract within the first thirty (30) days from the
> Service Contract Purchase Date, You will receive a full refund provided no
> claims have been made. (IX(a))

Sixty days appears only in the Florida section. Across the seven launch states:
Florida 60 days, Texas "before the thirty-first day", Wisconsin 30, North
Carolina 30 with the fee capped at $25 or 10%, and Ohio, South Carolina and
Pennsylvania on the base 30.

So **"thirty days" is honest in all seven states and "sixty days" would be wrong
in six of them.** Concept 7 is built on the 30 day figure with a state variation
qualifier.

## Not in the contract, and therefore not in the creative

None of the following appear anywhere in `AAS VSC 1 11-2022`. They are not
necessarily untrue, but nothing in the executed customer contract supports them,
so they are out of the artwork until there is a document that does.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| One year of Diminished Value protection, up to $5,000 | Zero occurrences of "diminish" in sixteen pages. **Needs a source.** |
| Openbay service scheduling and discounts | Zero occurrences. **Needs a source**, and separately needs the third-party-brand sign-off that is still an open item. |
| Protection Plus 4-in-1 (tire and wheel, key/remote, paintless dent, windshield) | Confirmed by Ryan, 27 Aug 2026, as **a paid upgrade in the website checkout flow, not part of the base VSC**. Correctly absent from the base coverage story. |
| "Terms up to 60 months" | Confirmed by Ryan as the **payment plan** term, not the coverage term. Out of the creative with the rest of the payment plan facts. |
| 0% payment plan up to 36 months, no credit check | A program fact, not a contract term. **Deliberately not printed**, per Ryan: Meta's Credit special ad category would restrict targeting and the claim is not worth it on prospecting. |

### Why Protection Plus mattered more than the others

It is not merely absent from the contract. The contract **excludes those exact
items by name**:

> VEHICLE EXTERIOR: Glass; Windows; Mirrors; Windshields (including any embedded
> electrical elements) ...
> ... Body Parts; Tires, Valve Stems and Wheels/Rims.

So a feature row reading "tire and wheel, windshield, paintless dent" beside VSC
coverage terms would have put inclusions on the ad that the contract the buyer
signs excludes in writing. Ryan's answer resolves it: Protection Plus is a
separate checkout upgrade. It can be sold on the site. It cannot be presented as
part of the coverage on a prospecting ad without saying it is an upgrade.
