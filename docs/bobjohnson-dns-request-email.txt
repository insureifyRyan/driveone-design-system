Subject: bobjohnsonauto.com sending authorisation - two options, neither is SMTP


Hi Mike,

We need to authorise one sending platform to send the customer follow-up series on
the dealership's behalf, from Northcountryrecalls@bobjohnsonauto.com.

There are two ways to do that. Both are below. Neither is SMTP, and neither asks
you to weaken anything.


FIRST, TO CLOSE OUT THE SMTP QUESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------
We tested it rather than assume. The mailbox has MFA enforced, and an SMTP
session has nowhere to carry a second factor - the password authenticates fine
and is then met with an Authenticator prompt that SMTP cannot answer.

So SMTP is not a policy we are asking you to revisit. It is closed by the MFA
you have correctly turned on, and we are not proposing app passwords or any
other way around it. That route is done.


OPTION A - FOUR DNS RECORDS  (our recommendation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
What it asks for: four DNS entries. Nothing else.

  - No mailbox access. Nobody gains the ability to read, send from, or
    sign in to any Microsoft 365 account.
  - No app registration, no OAuth consent, no admin approval in Microsoft.
  - No change to how your mail flows. Inbound and outbound through
    Microsoft 365 is untouched.
  - No credential of any kind is issued to us.

DNS records grant permission to nothing. They are public entries, in the same
category as the record that already points your mail at Microsoft, and all they
say is that one named platform may sign messages for this domain.

We recommend this one because it is the smaller grant of the two. If DNS for
bobjohnsonauto.com sits with your registrar or whoever maintains the website
rather than with you, this may be a forward rather than a task.

The records are listed at the end of this email.


OPTION B - APP REGISTRATION  (if you would rather keep it inside Microsoft)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
If you would prefer the mail go through your own tenant rather than an outside
platform, we can send through Microsoft Graph instead. This is the same
mechanism your existing system already uses.

What it needs:

  - An Entra ID app registration in your tenant with the Mail.Send
    application permission.
  - An application access policy scoping that permission to
    Northcountryrecalls@bobjohnsonauto.com only, so the registration cannot
    touch any other mailbox in the organisation.
  - The Tenant ID, Client ID and a client secret passed to us.

This grants more than Option A does - it is a credential, where DNS records are
not - but it is properly scoped, revocable by you at any time, and it keeps
everything inside Microsoft. It also leaves a copy of every message in the
mailbox's Sent Items, which Option A does not.

If you prefer this route, say so and we will send the exact steps.

THE RECORDS FOR OPTION A

PLEASE DO NOT CHANGE YOUR EXISTING SPF RECORD
--------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the only part of the request that could affect your current mail, and it
is avoidable entirely.

Your root domain almost certainly has an SPF record today, something like:

  v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all

Please leave it exactly as it is.

A domain may only have ONE SPF record. If a second one is added to the root, SPF
fails for the whole domain and your normal business email starts going to spam.

None of the records below touch it. The SPF entries here go on a "send"
subdomain that does not exist yet, which is specifically why the request is
built this way.

The "Name" values are the short form most DNS panels expect. If your panel wants
a fully qualified name instead, use the FQDN line. Please do not enter both, or
you will end up with bobjohnsonauto.com.bobjohnsonauto.com.

COMMON MISTAKES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Doubling the domain. Some panels append the domain automatically, so
    entering the FQDN in a panel that already appends it produces
    resend._domainkey.bobjohnsonauto.com.bobjohnsonauto.com, which will
    not verify.
  - The DKIM value wrapping. It must be one unbroken string. Copying from a
    formatted document can introduce spaces or line breaks.
  - Adding a second root SPF record. See the warning above. This is the only
    change here that could affect your existing mail, and none of these
    records require it.
